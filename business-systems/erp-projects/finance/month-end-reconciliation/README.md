# Month-End Financial Reconciliation & Closing Procedure

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to establish a standardized month-end closing process within SAP Business One to ensure that financial transactions, operational subledgers, and General Ledger (GL) balances are accurate, complete, and reconciled before the start of the subsequent accounting period.

This procedure provides end users with a structured sequence of activities to review outstanding transactions, reconcile subsidiary ledger balances with their corresponding GL control accounts, identify discrepancies, and resolve outstanding financial items.

## 2. Scope

This procedure covers the month-end financial reconciliation activities associated with the following business functions:

- Inventory Management
- Accounts Receivable (AR)
- Accounts Payable (AP)
- Customer Down Payments
- Banking and Checking Account Reconciliation
- Outstanding Checks and Payments
- General Ledger Review

## 3. Roles & Responsibilities

**Accounting / Finance Users**
- Execute assigned month-end reconciliation activities.
- Review financial and operational reports.
- Investigate discrepancies between subledger and GL balances.
- Document outstanding items and communicate unresolved issues.

**Accounting Manager / Controller**
- Review reconciliation results.
- Approve necessary adjusting journal entries.
- Verify that outstanding discrepancies have been resolved or documented.
- Authorize completion of month-end closing activities.

**Business Systems / ERP Support**
- Assist with system-generated reporting and troubleshooting.
- Investigate discrepancies related to ERP configurations, transaction posting, and system functionality.
- Support users in resolving application-related issues.

## 4. Month-End Closing Procedures

### Step 1: Inventory Reconciliation

**Objective:** Ensure that inventory quantities and valuation records agree with the corresponding General Ledger inventory accounts.

Activities:
1. Generate the Inventory Audit Report or equivalent inventory valuation report in SAP Business One.
2. Review inventory quantities and valuation by item and warehouse.
3. Identify inventory adjustments, negative inventory, and unusual valuation differences.
4. Generate the General Ledger report for the inventory control accounts.
5. Compare the inventory valuation total against the corresponding GL account balances.
6. Investigate discrepancies, including transactions posted directly to inventory GL accounts.
7. Document and resolve identified differences before completing the reconciliation.

**Expected Result:** Inventory valuation agrees with the corresponding General Ledger inventory control accounts.

### Step 2: Accounts Receivable Reconciliation

**Objective:** Ensure that customer outstanding balances agree with the Accounts Receivable control accounts.

Activities:
1. Generate the Accounts Receivable Aging Report as of the month-end date.
2. Review outstanding customer invoices and credit memos.
3. Identify overdue balances, unapplied payments, and unusual customer account balances.
4. Generate the General Ledger balances for the Accounts Receivable control accounts.
5. Compare the AR aging total against the corresponding GL control account balances.
6. Investigate discrepancies, including manual journal entries and transactions posted to incorrect accounts.
7. Document and resolve outstanding differences.

**Expected Result:** Accounts Receivable subsidiary ledger balances agree with the corresponding GL control accounts.

### Step 3: Accounts Payable Reconciliation

**Objective:** Ensure that vendor outstanding balances agree with the Accounts Payable control accounts.

Activities:
1. Generate the Accounts Payable Aging Report as of the month-end date.
2. Review outstanding vendor invoices and credit memos.
3. Identify overdue invoices, unapplied vendor payments, and outstanding balances.
4. Generate the General Ledger balances for the Accounts Payable control accounts.
5. Compare the AP aging total against the corresponding GL control account balances.
6. Investigate discrepancies, including manual journal entries and transactions posted to incorrect accounts.
7. Document and resolve outstanding differences.

**Expected Result:** Accounts Payable subsidiary ledger balances agree with the corresponding GL control accounts.

### Step 4: Customer and Vendor Down Payment Reconciliation

**Objective:** Ensure that outstanding down payment transactions are properly recorded, applied, and reconciled.

Activities:
1. Generate reports identifying outstanding customer and vendor down payment invoices.
2. Review down payment invoices that have not received corresponding payments.
3. Identify down payments that have been received or paid but have not yet been applied to final invoices.
4. Review associated payment transactions and outstanding document balances.
5. Verify that down payment balances agree with the appropriate GL accounts.
6. Investigate unapplied payments, open down payment documents, and discrepancies.
7. Document outstanding items requiring follow-up.

**Expected Result:** Down payment transactions and their associated GL balances are accurately recorded and reconciled, with outstanding items clearly identified.

### Step 5: Checking Account and Bank Reconciliation

**Objective:** Ensure that checking account balances in SAP Business One agree with bank statement balances and that outstanding checks and deposits are properly accounted for.

Activities:
1. Obtain the bank statement for the month-end period.
2. Review the checking account balance recorded in the General Ledger.
3. Compare bank statement transactions against recorded transactions in SAP Business One.
4. Identify outstanding checks that have been issued but have not cleared the bank.
5. Identify deposits in transit and other timing differences.
6. Review bank transfers, service charges, interest, and other transactions requiring recording.
7. Investigate unidentified or unreconciled transactions.
8. Prepare or complete the bank reconciliation.
9. Document outstanding checks and other reconciling items for follow-up in the subsequent month.

**Expected Result:** The checking account is reconciled to the bank statement, with outstanding checks, deposits in transit, and other reconciling items properly documented.

### Step 6: General Ledger Review

**Objective:** Verify that the financial balances and reconciliation results are complete and accurate.

Activities:
1. Generate the month-end Trial Balance.
2. Review the GL balances for inventory, AR, AP, bank accounts, and down payment accounts.
3. Verify that the corresponding subsidiary ledger reconciliations have been completed.
4. Review unusual account balances and unexpected variances.
5. Investigate manual journal entries and adjustments affecting control accounts.
6. Confirm that all required corrections have been approved and posted.
7. Document unresolved discrepancies and communicate them to the accounting manager.

**Expected Result:** The General Ledger reflects the reconciled financial position of the organization at month-end.

## 5. Reconciliation Documentation

Each reconciliation should include:

- Reporting period and reconciliation date.
- ERP subledger report and corresponding GL report.
- Subledger balance.
- General Ledger balance.
- Identified variance.
- Explanation of outstanding differences.
- Corrective actions taken.
- Preparer and reviewer information.
- Completion date and approval status.

## 6. Month-End Completion & Sign-Off

Before considering the month-end process complete:

1. Verify that all required reconciliations have been performed.
2. Confirm that identified discrepancies have been resolved or formally documented.
3. Verify that outstanding checks and deposits in transit are tracked for subsequent periods.
4. Confirm that down payment transactions have been reviewed.
5. Obtain the appropriate accounting management review and approval.
6. Confirm that the accounting period is ready for the next month's transactions, subject to the organization's period-closing and posting controls.

## 7. Exception Management

Any discrepancies identified during reconciliation must be documented and investigated.

Examples include:

- Inventory valuation differences.
- AR or AP aging balances that do not agree with GL control accounts.
- Unapplied customer or vendor payments.
- Unpaid or unapplied down payment invoices.
- Outstanding checks and unidentified bank transactions.
- Direct postings to GL control accounts.
- Incorrect account determination or transaction posting.

Issues that cannot be resolved during the closing process should be escalated to the accounting manager and documented for follow-up.

## 8. Expected Outcome

Completion of this procedure establishes a consistent month-end reconciliation process that promotes financial accuracy, improves visibility into outstanding transactions, strengthens internal controls, and ensures that accounting records are properly reviewed before proceeding into the next reporting period.
